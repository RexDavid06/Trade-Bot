**Consolidated findings from Phases 1–4 of the research laboratory.**

- **Dataset**: `data/eurusd_m5.csv`
- **Date range**: `2025-06-16 23:30 UTC` .. `2026-09-01 18:20 UTC`
- **Candles**: `90,000` M5 bars (~441.8 days / ~15 months)
- **Instrument**: EURUSD, M5
- **Generated by**: `python -m backtest.market_research.run_all`

## 1. Executive summary — "What does EURUSD M5 actually do here?"

The single most consistent result across **three independent studies** (momentum, trend persistence, mean reversion) is:

> **In this dataset EURUSD M5 is mean-reverting / anti-momentum.** Strong recent UP moves tend to be followed by *negative* forward returns (reversal); strong recent DOWN moves tend to be followed by *positive* forward returns (bounce). There is essentially **no momentum-continuation edge** at M5.

This is directionally consistent with why **V1 (EMA50/200 + RSI pullback)** and **V2 (trend + pullback + rejection)** were found to have **no directional edge**: both were continuation-style rules (buy pullbacks in an uptrend, expecting continuation). The underlying 5-minute behavior in this dataset is the opposite — it flips.

Two caveats that make this more "hypothesis" than "edge":

- The observed reversion effects, while statistically significant at these sample sizes and internally consistent, are **small in magnitude** (a few 0.1 pips average).
- Spread on this feed is **~0 for 46% of candles** (a feed artifact — see §3) and the last 200 bars are all zero-spread (possible stale end-of-feed). Before any live conclusion, these findings must be reproduced on a clean multi-year dataset and stress-tested against a **real spread and slippage model**.

## 2. Findings by study

### A. Momentum — reversal, not continuation
For all recent-move lookbacks (3→80): after the strongest **UP** bucket, forward return is **negative** and hit rate falls below 47% at H=1. After the strongest **DOWN** bucket, forward return is **positive** with hit ~50–51%.

| recent move (LB=3) | H=1 avg fwd (pips) | H=1 hit% |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 (strongest DOWN) | +0.10 | 50.4 |
| Q5 (strongest UP) | **−0.07** | **47.1** |

Sign-conditioned continuation (LB=3): after **up**, H=1 avg **−0.04** pips (47.5% hit); after **down**, H=1 avg **+0.04** pips. The reversal grows with horizon: after **up**, H=40 avg **−0.21** pips. Asymmetric, consistent reversal.

### B. Mean reversion — the strongest coherent signal
Distance = (close − trailing SMA) / trailing std (causal). Bars far below the mean (`<-2σ`) show positive forward returns with rising hit rate; bars far above the mean (`>+2σ`) show negative forward returns.

| dist bucket (SMA=10) | n | H=1 avg pips | H=1 hit% | H=3 hit% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `<-2σ` (below mean) | 3,925 | **+0.17** | **52.2** | 53.0 |
| `-1..-0.5σ` | 11,163 | +0.01 | 49.1 | 50.1 |
| `0.5..1σ` | 11,295 | −0.01 | 48.0 | 49.1 |
| `>2σ` (above mean) | 3,788 | −0.06 | 46.9 | 47.1 |

Fade-the-extreme summary (|dist| ≥ 1σ) is **positive on both sides** across every horizon (H=1→80), hit rates 51–52%:

| H | fade below (long) avg | long hit% | fade above (short) avg | short hit% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | +0.10 | 50.9 | +0.06 | 50.7 |
| 5 | +0.20 | 51.5 | +0.18 | 51.9 |
| 20 | +0.43 | 51.2 | +0.21 | 51.5 |
| 80 | +0.71 | 50.6 | +0.36 | 51.4 |

Sample per side ~23,000 bars. This is the most plausible **research candidate** for a future strategy — but see §6 limitations (needs out-of-sample validation and cost model before claiming edge).

### C. Breakout — the asymmetry again (DOWN breaks continue, UP breaks fail)
Bars that pierce the prior **N-candle low** (low breakout) are followed by *positive* forward returns; bars that pierce the prior **high** (high breakout) are followed by *negative* forward returns. So "breakouts" in this data are anti-momentum for the upside.

| Channel=160 | n | H=1 avg pips | H=1 hit% | H=5 hit% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| low breakout | 1,541 | +0.19 | **51.7** | 53.3 |
| inside | 86,730 | −0.00 | 48.6 | 49.3 |
| high breakout | 1,568 | **−0.14** | **46.4** | 44.9 |

Same pattern at channels 20/40/80. High-breakout failure is large and significant (H=3 ≈ −0.26..−0.51 pips). This strongly contradicts a "breakout continuation" hypothesis for the up-side in this period.

### D. Volatility — regime drives magnitude, not direction
Regimes (causal rolling ATR quantiles): **low 44.0%, normal 18.8%, high 37.2%** of bars. Median ATR ≈ **3.26 pips** (p10 1.95, p90 5.61).

No regime shows a directional hit rate far from 50% (all 48–49%). But regime strongly scales the **amount** price actually moves:

| regime | H=1 avg \|move\| (pips) |
|---|---|
| low | 1.37 |
| normal | 1.77 |
| high | 2.25 |

→ Volatility regime matters for **position sizing / risk**, not for direction.

### E. Sessions — no standout directional session, but New York is most active
| session | bars | H=1 hit% | H=1 avg \|move\| (pips) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asian | 26,223 | 48.7 | 1.26 |
| London | 18,738 | 49.2 | 1.82 |
| London/NY overlap | 15,064 | 48.2 | 2.10 |
| New York | 18,790 | 48.6 | **2.34** |
| OffHours | 11,185 | 47.8 | 1.50 |

No session shows a consistently directional edge. Sessions primarily differ in **volatility** (New York most, Asian least). Consistent with finding D.

### F. Trend persistence — trends do not continue, they revert
Using plain price-based trend (sign of net move over window 10–80):

| Trend window=10 | H=5 avg pips | H=5 hit% |
|---|---|---|
| down-trend | +0.11 | 50.6 |
| up-trend | **−0.11** | **47.9** |

Symmetrical flip at every window and horizon: an up-trend precedes a fall, a down-trend precedes a rise. Also, streak analysis (3–8 consecutive same-direction bars) shows no persistent continuation; the longest streaks (≥7) have small samples and unstable outcomes.

### G. Range / chop — wider recent range precedes larger forward moves
Range-index = (rolling high−low) / ATR. Forward **|move|** increases monotonically from the most compressed (Q1) to the widest (Q5) bucket:

| range-index quintile (RW=20) | H=1 avg \|move\| pips |
|---|---|
| Q1 (compressed) | 1.52 |
| Q3 | 1.72 |
| Q5 (widest) | 2.16 |

Foreshadows a **volatility-expansion** idea: after compression, price *does* expand more than average, but it expands **in both directions** (hit rate stays ~49–50%), so it is a volatility opportunity, not a directional edge.

- **Spread**: `0` for **41,451 candles (46.1%)**, spread uniformly across all weekdays (~45–48% each) → a **feed artifact**, not a real zero-spread market.
  - **Last 200 candles are all zero-spread** → possible stale/end-of-feed artifact. Need to re-verify against a fresh export.

**Interpretation**: the data is structurally clean (no duplicates/OHLC issues) but has a **degraded spread feed** (nearly half the bars report zero spread and the tail of the file is suspicious). This matters because any backtest using raw spread will drastically overstate achievable returns on 46% of bars.

## 7. Limitations — why these are hypotheses, not edges

1. **~15 months / 90k bars of one instrument is not a statistical foundation.** The 60/20/20 holdout (18k bars ≈ 3 months) is too short to be a meaningful OOS test on its own. This must be flagged rather than treated as sufficient.
2. **The spread feed is broken** (~46% zero-spread bars + a suspect 200-bar tail). Raw-price research ignores spread; translating a ~0.1-pip average edge into money requires a **realistic spread (~0.5–1 pip), slippage and commission** model, which could entirely erase these edges.
3. **All features are built causally (past-only) and forward windows are correctly de-nan'd**, but the studies share the **same forward-return machinery** — so the momentum/trend/reversion "reversal" findings are partly the same construct measured three ways. They corroborate but are not three fully independent proofs.
4. **No cost/slippage/commission or execution model** was applied (Phase 6 not yet done).
5. **Small-magnitude effects**: average reversion edge ≈ 0.1–0.2 pips at short horizons — small relative to typical EURUSD spread and volatility.
6. **Multiple-comparison caution**: many buckets × horizons were inspected; a few pseudo-significant cells are expected by chance even with no real effect. The *consistency* of the reversal across independent studies is what lends it credibility, not any single p-value.
7. **Regime splits and session splits** are preliminary; regime definitions (rolling ATR quantiles) and session boundaries (my chosen UTC cutpoints) are not hard-wired as "correct" — they are starting assumptions for the data to challenge.

## 8. What additional data would materially improve this research

1. **A larger, multi-year EURUSD M5 history** (ideally 3–5+ years, e.g. 2019–2026) so the 60/20/20 split yields a genuinely meaningful holdout and walk-forward windows can be formed.
2. **A clean, re-exported feed** that fixes the zero-spread artifact and the suspect 200-bar tail, so cost realism can be trusted.
3. **Multiple brokers/venues** for the same instrument, to test whether the observed mean-reversion pattern is broker-specific (the feed already shows broker-specific artifacts).
4. **A reliable spread/time-series** (real bid/ask spread history) rather than a static or zero-filled spread column, essential for Phase 6 cost sensitivity.
5. **Additional correlated pairs** (e.g. EURGBP, EURJPY, GBPUSD) M5 so any behavior found for EURUSD can be checked for family-level robustness rather than single-pair coincidence.
